# Dribbble research

## Research frame

This research was performed after the current-site audit. The aim was not to collect attractive agency shots, but to find patterns that could improve UI Forge Design's positioning, proof, project storytelling, service clarity, lead generation, and perceived craft. Twelve references were selected from searches spanning web-design agencies, creative development studios, digital-product studios, premium portfolios, and agency mobile experiences.

Dribbble is a visual-reference source, not usability evidence. Many shots show a single frame or concept rather than a tested production experience. Dates below are approximate where the public page did not expose an exact publication date. Motion is only discussed when the post or composition suggests it; it must be prototyped and tested before adoption.

## 1. Creative Agency Website — Good Code

- **Creator:** Good Code
- **URL:** [Creative Agency Website](https://dribbble.com/shots/26340265-Creative-Agency-Website)
- **Approximate date:** 2025; the description explicitly calls it the studio's “2025 edition”.
- **Why selected:** A credible example of a development-aware creative studio expressing confidence through a small number of decisive visual moves.
- **Hero treatment:** Oversized direct positioning, sharp geometry, black/electric-blue contrast, and a composition that immediately feels authored.
- **Typography:** Large grotesk display type with compact supporting copy and strong changes of scale.
- **Colour direction:** Near-black base, white copy, vivid blue as a controlled signal rather than decoration everywhere.
- **Layout/grid:** Hard-edged modular grid with intentional breaks and generous negative space.
- **Navigation:** Compact, visually subordinate to the proposition.
- **Portfolio treatment:** Work is positioned as the evidence for the studio's taste, with large project surfaces rather than small template cards.
- **Services:** Reduced to clear capabilities; the site does not lead with a long catalogue.
- **Social proof:** Confidence comes from work quality and studio specificity more than ornamental trust badges.
- **CTA strategy:** A direct conversation prompt integrated into the visual system.
- **Motion ideas:** The post references a Framer build; hard cuts, image reveals, and typographic transitions would suit the composition.
- **Visual motifs:** Sharp corners, high contrast, blue fields, oversized type.
- **Useful pattern:** One ownable accent, a bold point of view, and fewer competing messages.
- **Weak pattern:** High contrast and extreme scale can become tiring or obscure service detail.
- **UI Forge lesson:** Keep cobalt as a recognisable signal, but give it fewer, higher-value moments.
- **Do not copy:** The black-first palette or aggressive scale wholesale; UI Forge's strength is warmer and more approachable.

## 2. Obys — Viacheslav Olianishyn for Obys

- **Creator/studio:** Viacheslav Olianishyn for Obys
- **URL:** [Obys](https://dribbble.com/shots/27263832-Obys)
- **Approximate date:** Recent, approximately 2026; exact date was not exposed in the inspected public view.
- **Why selected:** The strongest example in the set of a coherent identity system where typography, grid, and storytelling all express one studio idea.
- **Hero treatment:** Concept-led rather than benefit-list-led; the opening behaves like an identity statement.
- **Typography:** Highly art-directed display type paired with disciplined functional text.
- **Colour direction:** Monochrome foundation with restrained tonal shifts.
- **Layout/grid:** Precise editorial system; irregularity is controlled by an underlying grid.
- **Navigation:** Minimal and identity-aligned.
- **Portfolio treatment:** Projects are framed as authored stories, not interchangeable thumbnails.
- **Services:** Secondary to the work and studio worldview.
- **Social proof:** Authority is communicated through consistency, selection, and craft.
- **CTA strategy:** Quiet invitations that do not interrupt the editorial experience.
- **Motion ideas:** Likely typographic sequencing and controlled project reveals; the public media was partly dynamic and should not be treated as a complete interaction specification.
- **Visual motifs:** Monochrome fields, oversized letterforms, strict alignment, editorial pacing.
- **Useful pattern:** Every component contributes to one memorable point of view.
- **Weak pattern:** Conceptual language and severe restraint can under-explain practical value to SME buyers.
- **UI Forge lesson:** Define a design premise first, then let type, spacing, motion, and project presentation reinforce it.
- **Do not copy:** Art-school abstraction that makes the offer or next step less legible.

## 3. Creative Boutique Studio Website — Klimt Creations

## Cross-reference findings

### Recurring patterns worth carrying forward

1. **An ownable proposition before a capability list.** The strongest references open with a point of view, then prove it. UI Forge currently explains a lot but does not establish one memorable phrase quickly enough.
2. **Work near the top.** Selected projects appear in the first two or three major homepage movements. A studio should not make buyers wait for evidence.
3. **Serif plus grotesk remains effective when roles are disciplined.** It creates editorial distinction without compromising functional clarity. UI Forge already has this asset and should evolve it rather than reset it.
4. **Restrained foundations outperform rainbow systems.** Monochrome or warm-neutral bases make one accent and the project imagery feel intentional.
5. **Project media is large and specific.** Strong pages avoid tiny browser mockups floating in empty gradients. They use close crops, responsive states, artefacts, and real context.
6. **Human presence is trust infrastructure.** Founder portraits, team imagery, or genuine client voices make boutique studios tangible.
7. **Proof is proximate to the claim.** Client names, outcomes, years, testimonials, or artefacts appear close to the statement they substantiate.
8. **Mobile is recomposed.** The best narrow layouts give project media full width, reduce copy, simplify navigation, and keep the CTA recoverable.
9. **Motion has a narrative job.** Useful motion establishes sequence, links an interaction to its result, or reveals project detail. It is not constant ambient movement.

### Patterns to reject or tightly control

- Generic 3D blobs, floating browser cards, blue-purple gradients, and abstract “AI agency” imagery.
- A dashboard/SaaS component language for a relationship-led creative service.
- Decorative metrics, client logos, satisfaction scores, and testimonials that cannot be verified.
- Dark cinematic styling on every page; it can make practical information cold and homogeneous.
- Bento grids used as a trend rather than to express meaningful content relationships.
- Vague positioning such as “we build digital experiences” without buyer, problem, or outcome.
- Scroll hijacking, cursor replacement, gesture-only galleries, auto-rotating testimonials, and motion that survives reduced-motion preferences.

## Implication for UI Forge Design

The research supports an evolution, not a visual reset. UI Forge already owns the beginnings of a distinctive editorial system: warm paper, ink, cobalt, an expressive serif, sharp rules, and plain language. The opportunity is to turn it into a proof-led system—fewer sections, larger and more credible project evidence, an accessible accent, visible founder presence, stronger outcome hierarchy, and selective motion. That synthesis becomes **Direction A — Crafted Evidence**, documented in the next two files.
