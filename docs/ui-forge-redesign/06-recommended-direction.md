# Recommended direction — Crafted Evidence

## Creative north star

**Crafted Evidence** is a proof-led editorial experience for a founder-led studio that designs and builds the same work. It should feel considered before it feels impressive, and convincing before it feels clever.

The redesign should make this proposition evident within moments:

> UI Forge helps Australian businesses turn important digital ideas into clear, well-made products—working directly with the person who designs and builds them, with practical advice and ownership built in.

This is a positioning framework, not final marketing copy.

## Why this direction wins

Crafted Evidence best balances premium presentation, creativity, design credibility, developer credibility, conversion clarity, maintainability, and realistic implementation.

It beats **Technical Atelier** because UI Forge's current audience includes non-technical founders and SME decision-makers who need confidence and clarity more than an engineering spectacle. Technical evidence remains an important layer inside cases, but it should not dominate the whole brand.

It beats **Human Workshop** because the studio also needs to prove high-end interface and development capability. Founder warmth should strengthen the system, not turn the business into a personality-led consultancy.

It also protects useful existing equity: the paper/ink/cobalt palette, serif/sans tension, rule-based editorial composition, plain-language advice, and direct-ownership promise. The change is structural and evidential, not cosmetic.

## Intended perception

Visitors should leave with five beliefs:

1. UI Forge is a senior, accountable partner—not an opaque agency layer.
2. The studio can both design and build production-quality work.
3. Recommendations are based on the client's situation, not a preferred platform sale.
4. Projects are thoughtfully scoped and responsibly handed over.
5. The next conversation will be useful even before a proposal is signed.

## Positioning hierarchy

1. **Outcome:** clear, effective digital products that the client can confidently own.
2. **Differentiator:** design and development stay connected through one founder-led practice.
3. **Method:** practical platform advice, staged decisions, visible craft, and honest communication.
4. **Capabilities:** websites/e-commerce, digital products, and ongoing care.
5. **Technology:** WordPress, Webflow, HubSpot, Shopify/Hydrogen, React/Next.js, React Native, and related tooling—shown as means, not the headline.

The current site often reverses this order by exposing a broad service catalogue before enough proof. The redesign should sell the decision quality first.

## Content principles

- **Claim, then proof.** Every major claim should be followed by a project, artefact, quote, result, or concrete operating detail.
- **Specific beats comprehensive.** Three relevant service paths are more credible than a list covering almost every digital build category.
- **Explain trade-offs.** Platform-neutral guidance is a differentiator; show the decisions and constraints, not just final pixels.
- **State uncertainty honestly.** Distinguish measured results, client-reported results, qualitative outcomes, and unavailable data.
- **Show the person.** Use the founder's real name, portrait, role, location, and involvement once approved.
- **Write for buyers.** Lead with situations and outcomes; keep technology for deeper evaluation.
- **Keep useful friction.** A fit-oriented contact experience should qualify without feeling like procurement paperwork.

## Visual composition

### Base system

Warm paper remains the default reading surface, deep ink anchors authority, and cobalt signals action and technical precision. Replace the current action blue with a darker accessible cobalt; reserve the brighter blue for large decoration or non-text detail.

The composition uses a strict grid, visible rules, strong alignment, and a small number of deliberate breaks. Large project media should occasionally extend beyond the text column. Density changes should reflect content importance: sparse for position and emotion, tighter for facts and evidence.

### Type

Retain Instrument Serif and Instrument Sans through concept validation. The pairing already fits the desired editorial/technical balance and avoids a needless brand reset. Limit serif use to short statements, case-study chapter openings, and selected numeric outcomes. Long-form copy, controls, navigation, labels, and data stay sans.

### Imagery

The redesign depends on a new evidence library:

- full-page and close-crop captures from real projects;
- desktop, tablet, and mobile states shown only when they reveal a decision;
- early sketches, architecture diagrams, content models, and component artefacts;
- contextual photography of the founder and working process;
- client-approved brand assets and before-state evidence;
- short, captioned motion clips for meaningful interactions.

Avoid stock collaboration photography, decorative laptops/phones repeated on every case, and abstract AI/3D imagery that cannot prove work.

## Signature design moves

1. **Evidence rail:** Compact labels attached to claims—role, scope, platform, ownership, measured result, or verification status.
2. **Project chapters:** Full-width visual interruptions that introduce a project problem, decision, or outcome instead of generic cards.
3. **Annotated artefacts:** Sparse notes explain why an interface or architecture choice matters.
4. **Dual-state surfaces:** Paper for explanation and ink for proof-heavy visual moments, with accessible foregrounds.
5. **Founder mark:** A repeatable small device that identifies direct founder involvement without pretending the business is a large team.

These devices must carry information. If they become ornamental, remove them.

## Hero direction

The hero should answer four questions quickly: What does UI Forge make? For whom? Why this studio? What should I do next?

Recommended composition:

- a concise, outcome-led headline with a clear Australian SME/product-team audience cue;
- one short supporting paragraph about connected design/development and confident ownership;
- primary CTA to discuss a project and secondary CTA to view work;
- one flagship project visual or controlled two-state sequence;
- a compact evidence line with truthful operating details;
- no rotating slogans, auto-playing showreel, or long capability sentence.

On mobile, the headline and CTA come first, the evidence line second, and the project visual third. Do not preserve a desktop split if it creates a tiny image or excessive first-screen height.

## Homepage cadence

The ideal experience has approximately eight major movements:

1. Position and flagship evidence.
2. Small trust/proof strip.
3. Two to four selected projects.
4. Buyer problems translated into outcomes.
5. Three service paths.
6. Founder-led advantage and delivery method.
7. Verified trust and useful artefacts.
8. Project fit/contact close.

Insights may appear as a compact coda if publishing will remain active. The homepage should not repeat the same ownership, platform, and process points in multiple differently styled sections.

## Portfolio expression

Work is the redesign's main credibility engine. Each project listing unit needs:

- project/client name and permission status;
- concise problem and UI Forge contribution;
- service/role and platform metadata;
- one dominant visual with a meaningful crop;
- outcome wording with an evidence label;
- direct path into the case study.

Case studies should alternate between editorial explanation and immersive evidence. They should reveal constraints, decisions, implementation, responsive behaviour, and handover—not just a sequence of polished mockups.

Until additional client projects are approved, the Work page should not pretend to have abundance. One deep internal case can remain, but the site should prioritise acquiring two or three external, permissioned cases before using a sparse portfolio-first design.

## Trust model

Trust should be cumulative rather than confined to one section:

- Founder identity in the first half of the homepage.
- Clear Australian location and remote working model.
- Named work, role boundaries, dates, and client context.
- Honest project-status and result labels.
- Verified testimonials with source/date/permission records.
- Specific ownership, hosting, support, and handover commitments.
- Accessible, performant execution as demonstrated practice.
- Complete production-ready privacy and terms content.

Client logos must never be decorative filler. A logo appears only when there is a real, permissioned relationship and its meaning is clear.

## Motion and interaction

Motion should communicate continuity and causality:

- 120–180ms for hover, focus, and press feedback;
- 180–240ms for small disclosures and section entry;
- optional, user-controlled project media for complex interaction demonstrations;
- no scroll hijacking, kinetic background loops, custom cursor, or essential information that appears only on hover;
- `prefers-reduced-motion` should remove transforms and sequencing rather than merely shorten them.

The current global ban on all transitions protects accessibility but also removes useful feedback. Replace it with a small approved motion token set and explicit reduced-motion behaviour.

## Mobile translation

Mobile is a separate composition using the same system:

- keep hero lines short enough to scan at 36–48px rather than forcing desktop display scale;
- retain the current accessible menu fundamentals—focus management, Escape close, scroll lock—and simplify its visual hierarchy;
- let project media use the full viewport width while text retains safe gutters;
- replace dense multi-column specifications with ordered definition lists;
- avoid hidden horizontal galleries; if used, expose controls, count, labels, and a vertical fallback;
- stack proof immediately after the claim it supports;
- use 44px minimum interactive targets and persistent but non-obstructive CTA recovery;
- remove decorative material before shrinking essential copy;
- keep sticky elements limited to a compact header or contextual case-study index.

## Content and asset prerequisites

Before high-fidelity design, secure:

1. Founder name, approved biography, portrait, and preferred level of visibility.
2. Two to four external projects with client permission and role clarity.
3. Source files/captures for responsive states and process artefacts.
4. Measured or client-reported outcomes with provenance.
5. At least two verified testimonials before publishing a testimonial module or route.
6. Approved privacy and terms documents.
7. Clear service boundaries, typical project fit, timing, and support model.

If these cannot be secured, the concept must be simplified rather than padded with synthetic proof.

## Implementation fit

The existing Next.js App Router, TypeScript, Tailwind v4, `next/image`, metadata/JSON-LD patterns, and typed content modules can support this direction. The redesign does not require a framework change or heavy animation library. It does require:

- tokenising colour, type, spacing, grid, and motion more explicitly;
- formalising reusable project/evidence content schemas;
- auditing heading structures and interactive target sizes;
- deciding whether Contentful becomes the source of truth or remains only a dependency/proxy;
- defining image crops and responsive art direction;
- adding analytics only after measurement goals and consent requirements are decided.

## Success criteria

The direction is working if moderated review or testing shows that target visitors can:

- describe UI Forge's offer and difference after one short homepage scan;
- find relevant work before encountering a large service catalogue;
- identify who they will work with and what they will own;
- distinguish verified outcomes from general claims;
- choose an appropriate service path without understanding the technology stack;
- complete the project enquiry comfortably on a 390px viewport;
- navigate the experience with keyboard, reduced motion, and 200% zoom;
- experience comparable clarity and craft at mobile, tablet, and desktop widths.
