# Redesign directions

These directions respond to the same business problem in materially different ways. They are not colour variants. Each changes the site's hierarchy, visual character, proof model, and customer perception.

## Direction A — Crafted Evidence

### Strategic idea

Turn UI Forge's current editorial identity into a proof-led studio experience. The concept communicates that thoughtful design and dependable engineering come from the same hands. It should feel exact, human, and quietly confident—not like a large agency, freelancer template, or SaaS product.

The desired customer perception is: “This is a senior, hands-on partner who can explain the trade-offs, make the work beautiful, and deliver something I will actually own.”

### Visual language

- **Typography:** Retain the Instrument Serif/Instrument Sans pairing initially. Use serif for short authored statements and selected case-study moments; use sans for navigation, body, data, and interfaces.
- **Scale:** Fewer but stronger changes in scale. A large, controlled hero; compact evidence labels; comfortable editorial body text.
- **Layout and grids:** Twelve columns on large screens, six on tablet, four on mobile. Strong shared axes with occasional project imagery that breaks the text container.
- **Spacing:** Generous section separation, tighter internal groups, and substantially fewer stacked homepage sections.
- **Colours:** Warm paper, deep ink, muted greys, and a darker accessible cobalt for action. Bright cobalt remains a decorative highlight only.
- **Surfaces:** Mostly flat paper/ink fields. Project-specific colour can enter through real work.
- **Cards:** Cards only when the content is genuinely repeatable. Work uses editorial project frames; services use simple bordered rows or panels.
- **Borders/radius:** Fine rules, squared or 2–6px corners, no universal rounded-card treatment.
- **Iconography:** Bespoke, compact line icons or typographic markers; avoid generic illustration packs.
- **Imagery:** Real project captures, responsive states, process artefacts, the founder, and occasional documentary details.
- **Case studies:** Large evidence-led chapters combining context, decisions, artefacts, implementation detail, and outcomes.
- **Motion:** Controlled reveal, media-state transitions, and tactile feedback. No scroll hijacking or permanent ambient motion.
- **Decoration:** Registration marks, annotations, project numbering, crop details, and small “made/verified” cues used sparingly.

### Hero concept

A concise outcome-led statement occupies roughly two thirds of the canvas. A flagship project frame or sequence occupies the remaining area and proves design/development range. A compact evidence line sits beside the primary CTA: founder-led, Australia-based, clear ownership, and a truthful project/result cue. The design should avoid presenting six capabilities before the first proof.

### Homepage rhythm

Position → evidence → selected work → buyer problems/outcomes → three service pillars → founder advantage → process artefacts → verified trust → fit/contact. Insights become a compact optional coda rather than another full homepage chapter.

### Portfolio style

Two to four featured projects in varied editorial compositions, each with a clear client/project label, problem, contribution, and outcome status. No generic mockup wallpaper. Every image should demonstrate an interface decision, responsive behaviour, or result.

### Trust strategy

Trust is attached to claims: real founder identity and portrait, named client/project context where permitted, honest outcome wording, ownership/support terms, real delivery artefacts, accessibility/performance checks, and verified testimonials only. The existing testimonial publication threshold remains.

### Risks

- The direction fails if project content stays thin; editorial restraint exposes missing proof.
- It can feel overly quiet if project media and founder photography are weak.
- Decorative annotations can become affectation if they do not carry useful information.
- Retaining the current type and palette demands disciplined evolution so the result feels new enough.

### Inspiration mapping

- Good Code: decisive accent and authored positioning.
- Obys: one coherent typographic/grid premise.
- Klimt Creations and Anna: warmth, restraint, and boutique confidence.
- Oripio and Topnotch: work and human proof brought forward.
- Zain's Studio: mobile re-composition and proof sequencing.

## Direction B — Technical Atelier

### Strategic idea

Present UI Forge as a small engineering atelier: a studio where interface craft, performance, architecture, and implementation details are visible. This direction emphasises developer credibility and would appeal to product teams, technical founders, and buyers replacing fragile or locked-in systems.

The desired customer perception is: “This studio understands the hard implementation details other design agencies avoid.”

### Visual language

- **Typography:** Neutral grotesk for most copy, monospace for annotations/data, and a narrow display face for emphasis. Serif use is minimal or removed.
- **Scale:** Dense technical labels against a small number of oversized statements.
- **Layout and grids:** Exposed grid, modular technical panels, split views, sticky specifications, and interface states.
- **Spacing:** More compact than Direction A, but governed by strong rhythm.
- **Colours:** Ink/charcoal foundation, cool grey, off-white, and electric blue or acid-lime status accents.
- **Surfaces:** Layered dark panels and light “documentation” sheets.
- **Cards:** Specification panels, architecture summaries, benchmark strips, and annotated media.
- **Borders/radius:** Square corners, 1px rules, visible grid lines.
- **Iconography:** Monoline system and code-like status glyphs.
- **Imagery:** Product interfaces, code/architecture fragments, QA views, audit artefacts, and device states.
- **Case studies:** A technical narrative with requirements, decisions, stack, constraints, performance, and delivery evidence.
- **Motion:** State transitions, before/after sliders with accessible controls, terminal-like progress only when truthful, and diagram sequencing.
- **Decoration:** Coordinates, build stamps, schema lines, and functional annotations.

### Hero concept

A split hero pairs a precise promise with a working project specimen: responsive states, annotated components, or a before/after system. A compact “delivery spec” lists the kinds of outcomes UI Forge can substantiate—ownership, maintainability, performance, accessibility, and integrated design/development.

### Homepage rhythm

Position → live specimen → engineering proof → selected systems → capability matrix → delivery method → technical case study → founder credentials → project fit.

### Portfolio style

Projects are documented as systems. Cards show a visual and a small technical summary; case studies reveal architecture and implementation decisions alongside brand/interface work.

### Trust strategy

Use audits, performance traces, accessibility evidence, code-quality process, maintainability decisions, and explicit handover/support terms. Avoid pretending that automated metrics alone equal business impact.

### Risks

- It can feel cold, cyber-styled, or enterprise-oriented to the core SME audience.
- Dense technical detail may obscure the emotional and commercial value of design.
- The visual language is easy to turn into a generic developer-portfolio trope.
- Strong evidence requires permission to expose implementation artefacts.

### Inspiration mapping

- Good Code: strong technical confidence and hard-edged geometry.
- Obys: disciplined system thinking.
- Zonda Studio: atmosphere, grain, and dark restraint.
- Jigarr: cinematic project frames.
- Moksha Labs: modular energy, used in moderation.

## Direction C — Human Workshop

### Strategic idea

Frame UI Forge as the calm, collaborative alternative to agency layers and platform lock-in. The founder/client relationship, plain-language guidance, and sustainable ownership are the central product. This direction is less visually severe and most directly reassures non-technical SME buyers.

The desired customer perception is: “I will work with a thoughtful expert who listens, explains choices, and will not disappear after launch.”

### Visual language

- **Typography:** Warm serif for key statements and a highly legible humanist sans for the working interface.
- **Scale:** Conversational rather than monumental; emphasis comes from rhythm and photography.
- **Layout and grids:** Loose editorial composition, paired stories, process timelines, and workshop-like artefact clusters.
- **Spacing:** Generous and calm, with denser practical guidance modules.
- **Colours:** Cream, charcoal, muted forest, clay, and a restrained terracotta action colour.
- **Surfaces:** Paper-like layers, subtle tonal sections, and occasional image-backed panels.
- **Cards:** Softer rectangles for stories, FAQs, and collaboration stages; not a wall of equal cards.
- **Borders/radius:** 8–16px only where softness helps; rules remain crisp around functional information.
- **Iconography:** Handled as small drawn marks or simple humanist line icons.
- **Imagery:** Founder portrait, workspace/process photography, client collaboration, sketches, whiteboards, and real artefacts.
- **Case studies:** Client-centred narratives told through the original situation, working relationship, turning points, result, and ownership after launch.
- **Motion:** Gentle crossfades, photo sequencing, and tactile hover/press feedback.
- **Decoration:** Captions, handwritten-style annotations used rarely, material textures, and editorial pull quotes.

### Hero concept

A founder-led statement and portrait/work image establish accountability immediately. The supporting copy explains who the studio is for and how working directly changes the outcome. The primary CTA starts a low-pressure conversation; selected work is visible within the first viewport transition.

### Homepage rhythm

Founder promise → client outcome/proof → selected work → common anxieties → collaborative method → service paths → testimonial story → ownership/support promise → conversation.

### Portfolio style

Projects are framed around client context and the working relationship. Process artefacts and before/after explanations carry as much weight as polished finals.

### Trust strategy

Use the founder's full identity, face, location, operating principles, communication expectations, verified quotes, and post-launch commitment. Plain-language answers address price, ownership, timeline, platform choice, and support.

### Risks

- It can resemble a generic coach/consultancy if technical and visual evidence is insufficient.
- Warm palettes and rounded surfaces are common and can weaken distinctiveness.
- Over-indexing on the founder may create capacity concerns or make future growth harder.
- Documentary imagery must be genuinely good; staged stock imagery would damage trust.

### Inspiration mapping

- Klimt Creations and Anna: warm, quiet confidence.
- Topnotch: memorable human presence.
- Oripio: people plus selected work and FAQ.
- Opndoo: proof close to the proposition.
- Zain's Studio: direct mobile conversion flow.

## Comparative assessment

| Criterion | Crafted Evidence | Technical Atelier | Human Workshop |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Premium presentation | High | High | Medium–high |
| Design credibility | High | Medium–high | Medium–high |
| Developer credibility | High | Very high | Medium |
| SME trust and clarity | High | Medium | Very high |
| Differentiation | High | High, but trope risk | Medium–high |
| Conversion clarity | High | Medium–high | High |
| Fit with current equity | Very high | Medium | Medium–high |
| Content burden | High project-proof burden | Very high technical-proof burden | High human-content burden |
| Implementation realism | High | Medium | High |
| Long-term flexibility | High | Medium–high | Medium |

**Recommendation:** Direction A — Crafted Evidence. It combines the best parts of the existing brand with stronger commercial proof and can flex between human, visual, and technical content without forcing the studio into one narrow persona.
